# Sources and evidence limits

Reviewed 2026-10-11. These notes paraphrase relevant material; they do not reproduce the articles. The facilitation rules are design choices informed by the sources and the user's preferences, not a validated ADHD intervention.

## Practical sources used

- [Jeff Copper, DIG Coaching: Brainstorming—An ADHD Power Tool](https://digcoaching.com/brainstorming-an-adhd-power-tool/), March 3, 2025. Coaching advice emphasizes generating possibilities before judging them and asking collaborators about experiences. Adaptation: separate capture from evaluation, with an AI scribe that does not invent personal experiences. The article does not establish a particular timer duration or clinical efficacy.
- [Dr. Sharon Saline: Organizing Tips for ADHD Adults Who Feel Unmotivated](https://www.drsharonsaline.com/archived-blog-posts/2022/05/organizing-tips-for-adhd-adults-who-feel-unmotivated), May 18, 2022. Practical advice includes small starting actions, manageable categories, supportive assistance, visible organization, and less punitive self-talk. Adaptation: a concise checkpoint, neutral language, and one concrete next action.
- [Mishal Saeed and Adam Ly, CHADD: From Chaos to Clarity—Using Mind Maps to Navigate Adult ADHD](https://chadd.org/adhd-news/adhd-news-adults/attention-monthly-from-chaos-to-clarity-using-mind-maps-to-navigate-adult-adhd/), Attention Magazine, October 2024. The indexed article describes externalizing ideas and visual connections, while acknowledging limited research specifically on mind maps for ADHD. Adaptation: optional compact maps alongside a text alternative, not a claim that maps work better for everyone. The originally supplied article URL returned 403; the same article was available through its indexed CHADD page.
- [Lisa Colledge: How to Design a Brainstorm for Autistic and ADHD Thinkers—and Everyone Else](https://www.lisacolledgeconsulting.com/podcast01/how-to-design-a-brainstorm-for-autistic-and-adhd-thinkers-and-everyone-else), July 15, 2025. Practitioner guidance recommends preparation, quiet idea generation, written and verbal contributions, and room for follow-up ideas over one or two days. Adaptation: group participation options and asynchronous additions. That is not a requirement to postpone ranking every idea for two days.

## Academic context and boundaries

- [Chan et al.: Central Executive Training for ADHD—Impact on Organizational Skills at Home and School](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37439737/) ([PMC10615842](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10615842/)), 2023. The randomized trial studied 73 children aged 8–13 receiving ten-week training interventions. Its organizational-skills findings do not test this AI workflow, adult brainstorming, or the suggested timeboxes.
- [Bikic et al.: Organizational Skills Training for Children with ADHD—Study Protocol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34715906/) ([PMC8556963](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8556963/)), 2021. This publication describes a planned trial for children, not its completed outcomes. It cannot establish adult benefit or validate this skill.
- [Shrestha et al.: Non-pharmacologic Management of ADHD in Children and Adolescents—A Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7082245/), 2020. Pediatric review, retained as background; its indexed title and scope were verified, but the full article was not accessible in this review. It is not evidence for specific adult facilitation rules.

The user-supplied [ADDitude organization article](https://www.additudemag.com/how-to-get-organized-with-adhd/) returned 403 and was not used as verified evidence. The supplied AI overviews were treated as leads to check, not authoritative sources. Their Reddit, Medium, video, and skill-directory links were not independently reviewed for this update.

## Interpretation

Low-friction capture, one working note, parked ideas, restart checkpoints, and user-chosen short rounds are workflow adaptations. Their value should be assessed from the user's actual experience. Preserve useful structure without assuming everyone with ADHD has the same preferences, abilities, creativity, or difficulties. Avoid presenting the skill as therapy, a diagnosis, or a substitute for clinical care.
