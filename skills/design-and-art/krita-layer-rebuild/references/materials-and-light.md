# Materials and light

Read when a reconstruction depends on material appearance, lighting or natural surface structure. Choose the relevant procedures; do not apply every material recipe to every object. Most procedures are source-based native adaptations pending trials. The Revoy continuation supplies bounded hair improvements and cloth failure diagnoses, identified below. Page citations identify inspected sources rather than guaranteed implementations.

## Separate form, light and surface

Start from a matched silhouette and major planes. Record local color, lit and shadow families, light direction, cast-shadow receiver and edge behavior. Within each object's ownership, separate broad form/color, material response, identifiable surface details and dependent effects. Do not require five equally spaced shading bands: the reference may omit a visible core shadow or use deliberately simplified lighting.

Establish the dominant light/shadow relationship before small contrast. Reflected light belongs to the shadow family unless the observed reference says otherwise. A specular highlight responds to the light/view/material relationship; it need not sit at the center of the lit area. Compare local temperatures from the target rather than assuming warm light always means cool shadow. Penumbra depends on light extent and geometry; a soft shadow is not simply light scattering around an edge.

**Check:** inspect thumbnail value groups, then terminators, cast edges and contacts at native size. Toggle surface texture: the underlying volume must still read and match. Recoloring local pigment must not recolor an independently owned light source or erase its effects indiscriminately.

Sources: Mitch Leeuwe, *How To Learn Digital Painting*, PDF 11–14; Okku, *The Key to Captivating Character Illustration*, lessons 10, 16 and 24; existing Proko/Bucci/Gurney synthesis. Lesson titles/captions supply methods, not continuous video observation.

## Skin and eyes

Keep large facial planes and color transitions separate from freckles, wrinkles and other identified marks. Preserve eyelid overlaps and mouth/nostril boundaries while softening local transitions. Avoid a whole-face blur that removes structure. For a visible eye, distinguish lid, iris, pupil, rim, lid shadow, waterline and glints according to what the reference actually shows; do not add anatomically plausible detail that changes a simplified style.

**Check:** alter a cheek's broad shadow without moving a freckle or eye; edit one glint without changing the iris. A glint depends on lighting, and an eye-position edit can require lid/face changes. Inspect both face-scale expression and enlarged transitions.

Sources: Okku lessons 16-1 through 16-3; *Adobe Photoshop Tricks and Tips*, edition 19 (2024), PDF 56–61. The book's frequency-separation workflow motivates separate roles; this skill does not extract reference frequencies or reuse its image-specific blur/subtraction parameters.

## Cloth and wood

For cloth, establish garment silhouette, seams, attachments and tension/compression regions. Draw folds as connected ridges and troughs that explain the fabric: a sleeve bends around an arm, a belt compresses cloth, and a hem has thickness. Fit pattern direction, scale and distortion to the fold surface. Keep each promised motif, seam, stitch or wear mark independently owned. A weave field alone does not satisfy per-thread editing.

Separate stripe/pattern direction from the direction of the cloth's roll and crease. Use local tones along the upper light, front turn and underside shadow; a whole-curve average can flatten these changes. Choose broad unequal planes and restrained fibers according to the source. Inspect actual decorative paint inside a motif envelope: neutral cloth stored there can block receiver refinements or change during a supposed pattern-only recolor.

For wood, establish object faces and curvature before grain. Align grain and knots with the visible surface; vary local scale and contrast instead of distributing identical strokes everywhere. Separate grain from broad volume shading and contact wear.

**Check:** remove or recolor a motif without changing fold shading; reshape a fold and update its mapped pattern. Change a broad wooden shadow without rotating its grain. A straight texture over a curved form or a slab-like fold is a construction failure, not a need for more noise.

Sources: Leeuwe PDF 20, 34; Okku lessons 16 and 18; *Photoshop Tricks and Tips* PDF 74–79. Photoshop recoloring retains baked folds; our adaptation constructs folds natively. Pure-black multiplication can erase detail and is not a universal recoloring solution.

Bounded Revoy findings: whole-curve glazes flattened scarf tones, and narrow vertical plane strokes produced a comb-like surface despite improved stripe continuity. A complete cloth treatment and pattern-only controls remain unvalidated; see [tutorial evidence](tutorial-reproduction.md#attribution-and-bounded-evidence).

## Metal and gems

For metal, divide the object into observed faces or curved bands. Place strong light/dark environmental transitions where the surface bends, then add specular accents and local wear. Distinguish scratched paint, exposed metal and recess dirt. Contact/convex wear should not become uniform speckles over every face. A nonmonotonic color curve can produce a metallic graphic treatment, but does not establish a physical metal model.

For gems, record thickness, facet boundaries, top/bottom relations, reflected accents and transmitted surroundings. The course's dark-upper/light-lower example is conditional on that depicted jewel and lighting. Retain facet geometry and separate accents; changes to receiver/background can require new transmission and reflection paint.

**Check:** toggle the strongest accent and inspect whether facet/band structure remains. Changing one facet preserves adjacent facets; moving the object updates reflected surroundings and contact shadows. Saturating every edge with white is not a substitute for matching observed reflections.

Sources: Okku lesson 18; *Photoshop Tricks and Tips* PDF 62–67; existing Robertson *How to Render* weathering synthesis (PDF 240). Preserve stylization when it is in the target.

## Glass, liquid and translucent solids

Separate container silhouette, front/back rims, thickness, transmitted view, reflections, contents and liquid level. Glass often reveals and distorts its surroundings; do not fill it with opaque highlight bands. Draw the cork or opaque insert as its own material. For translucent jelly or skin, distinguish light entering and leaving the material from an emitting core; subsurface appearance is not automatically a glow effect.

**Check:** move the container over a changed background and rebuild the affected transmitted/reflected region. Changing liquid level leaves container geometry intact and updates meniscus, contents and optical effects. An unchanged interior patch proves only that pixels moved, not that refraction stayed coherent.

Sources: Leeuwe PDF 15–16, 22, 35; existing Robertson reflected-water synthesis (PDF 222). Hidden surroundings are explicitly inferred. Refraction changes ray direction; color dispersion is a distinct effect.

## Hair, fur, feathers and scales

Construct hair as an attached volume, then locks following growth/flow, then selected strands. Keep back masses subordinate when the reference does, and map highlights to the changing form. Fur clumps follow anatomy and vary at silhouette edges. Feathers and scales follow rows, overlap order and surface direction; do not scatter identical motifs without the supporting volume.

Bind each flow guide to its own mass or lock. Choose width and length separately: allow a gesture to cross incidental texture, but stop or change it at a true light/shadow plane or occlusion. Use local seed/station tones instead of one average for the entire curve. Vary starts, lengths and selected fine accents while retaining broad unequal facets. Keep source-observed exterior wisps explicitly owned; a coarse body mask can clip them away.

**Check:** hide fine strands/scales: the large silhouette and volume still match. Edit a visible lock, clump, feather or scale independently and repair its overlap and attached highlights. A helmet-shaped mass covered with straight lines requires volume correction first.

Sources: Leeuwe PDF 28–29, 31; Okku lessons 4, 17 and 26. Character-specific attachment and expression checks are in [character-illustration.md](character-illustration.md).

Bounded Revoy finding: longer, locally toned owner-bound Bristles paths reduced capsule chains across seven hair masses. Excessively uniform fibers still softened the source's broad facets. The tested widths, lengths and density belong to that output scale, not universal defaults; inspect a small saved proof before extending them.

## Water, vegetation, rocks and clouds

Water needs broad depth/color and reflected masses before wave breaks and foam. Fit reflections to their source and visible vertical extent; interrupt them with observed horizontal surface structure rather than a repeated ladder. Near foam and distant ripples need different scales.

Build vegetation from clustered masses, branch/leaf direction and holes. Use individual nearby blades when promised, and declare distant texture aggregation explicitly. Rocks need plane changes before grain. Snow follows support and coverage boundaries; clouds and smoke have connected volumes, varied edges and internal light/shadow, rather than uniformly blurred outlines.

**Check:** inspect grouping, silhouette gaps and depth at thumbnail scale. Move a branch or rock and update overlaps and shadows. Change the sky behind a cloud without carrying sky-colored edge paint. Individually editable blade promises require individual ownership, even when the studied source works in masses.

Sources: Leeuwe PDF 25, 37–39, 40–43; Okku lessons 18, 26 and 27. Lighting depth rules inform diagnosis; the faithful master retains observed exceptions.
