# Character illustration

Read for human/animal characters, expression, hair, clothing and character-centered composition. Adapted from the 50 supplied caption files for Okku's *The Key to Captivating Character Illustration* and the Leeuwe study. Captions do not supply all demonstrated brush motions; no continuous course-video viewing or new painting test is claimed.

## Record the style before applying anatomy rules

Measure the depicted head/body ratio, face outline, eye spacing/tilt, nose/mouth placement, limb proportions and depiction density. Identify which features are shapes, which are outlines and which are painted plane transitions. The reference can simplify anatomy or exaggerate proportions deliberately. Supplemental anatomical/material references can explain an ambiguous form but must not replace the faithful target with a preferred style.

Create a character scaffold with torso/pelvis direction, joint positions, face landmarks, clothing envelope and contacts. Compare expression as coordinated eye, brow and mouth relationships; adjusting one feature alone can change the expression. Keep intended asymmetry. Label an uncertain malformed feature for a possible corrected version.

**Check:** silhouette, face landmark ratios and pose agree before interior rendering. At thumbnail scale, character emphasis and expression remain consistent; at face scale, brow/lid/mouth spacing is checked explicitly.

Sources: Okku lessons 4-1, 4-2, 5 and 7; Leeuwe PDF 26–27, 33–34. These construction tests serve reconstruction, not unsolicited character redesign.

## Build hair from attachment and flow

Locate the skull under the hair, roots/parting and attachments such as a ponytail base. Divide front, side and rear masses by actual overlaps. Draw the main lock directions and tapered silhouette breaks. Add light/shadow across the mass before finer strands, with highlights following curvature and behind-facing locks using the reference's contrast.

Keep a visible lock's silhouette, tone, distinguishing strand and accessory under a traceable owner. A hairpin may be a separate object with attachment dependencies. Moving a ponytail changes its attachment to the head; translating its tip alone is a different edit.

**Check:** hide strands and confirm attached volume. Remove one lock without erasing the face or unrelated rear hair. Check vacated regions and the root connection after a move. Do not fill every gap between locks with background-colored paint.

Sources: Okku lessons 4 and 17-1/17-2; Leeuwe PDF 31.

## Coordinate line, fill and plane depiction

Close the intended silhouette before filling. Inspect openings, narrow fingers, accessories and overlaps individually. Keep contour, base color and interior depiction separate when the style uses all three. A small selection expansion may prevent seams, but the source's pixel amount is not portable across dimensions or edge styles; calibrate against the actual contour.

Start with dominant light and shadow families, then subordinate intermediate planes and material accents. Change contour weight according to the observed style and overlap; dark outer/lighter inner lines are a source example, not a universal rule. When a line is softened into a plane, retain ownership and inspect whether the important boundary still reads.

**Check:** hide tone layers to inspect base closure; then hide outlines to inspect plane consistency. Filling does not leak into a sibling object or seal an intended opening. Interior shading supports the actual pose rather than creating a new contour.

Sources: Okku lessons 9, 10, 15 and 16. Source-layer merging is adapted to retained ownership in this skill.

## Treat clothes, accessories and props as connected objects

Locate seam directions, compression at belts/cuffs, thickness at hems, contact with hands and occlusion behind the body. Distinguish a garment's base from patterns, fold shading, seams and fasteners. Define hand/prop contact on both owners: moving a book can require fingers to move or the pose to change. A copied accessory instance must preserve its own angle, size and perspective rather than inherit an unrelated frontal view.

**Check:** edit a single motif or fastener independently. Recolor clothing while preserving pattern identity and material shading; any palette-dependent sheen must be reconsidered. Moving a prop leaves coherent grip/contact, updated cast effects and a repaired exposed region. Use [materials-and-light.md](materials-and-light.md) for cloth, metal, skin and gems.

Sources: Okku lessons 16, 18 and 25; Leeuwe PDF 20, 34. PSD readability and imported node counts do not validate these edits.

## Maintain character/background relationships

Record camera height/projection, focal scale, foreground/background order and the reference's strongest contrasts. Inspect almost-touching edges and merged silhouettes. In the faithful master, retain actual composition and tangencies; propose intentional separation only in a named corrected version. Different scene layers can use different lighting for artistic reasons—preserve the observed exception rather than enforcing one classroom formula.

**Check:** at thumbnail size, compare the character's emphasis, background masses and gap rhythm. At full size, inspect contact, edge contamination and foreground crossings. Effects cannot compensate for a wrong camera or hidden limb.

Sources: Okku lessons 6, 8, 12–13, 21–24. For scene recoloring and finish, read [layout-and-effects.md](layout-and-effects.md).
